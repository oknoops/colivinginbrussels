# Brussels vs Antwerp: Which Belgian City Should You Live In?

Belgium's two most magnetic cities pull newcomers in different directions. **Brussels** is the international capital — EU institutions, endless nationalities, French-speaking daily life. **Antwerp** is the stylish, Flemish port city — fashion, diamonds, and a big-town confidence. Both are great. Here's an honest comparison to help you choose.

## The quick verdict

- **Choose Brussels if:** you want an international, English-friendly bubble, work with the EU/NGOs/tech, value being central in Europe, and like a bigger, more diverse capital.
- **Choose Antwerp if:** you want a cooler, more design-led scene, prefer a Flemish (Dutch-speaking) environment, love fashion and nightlife, and want a slightly more compact, walkable city.

## Vibe & culture

**Brussels** is a patchwork of 19 communes and a third-foreign population — surreal, multicultural, a little chaotic, and endlessly interesting. It doesn't try to charm you; it grows on you. **Antwerp** is more immediately cool: a famous fashion academy, a gorgeous railway station, a buzzing port-city swagger, and a strong local identity. Antwerp feels like a big town that thinks it's a metropolis (in the best way); Brussels feels like a small metropolis that pretends it's a town.

## Language

This is a real difference:
- **Brussels** is officially bilingual but functions largely in **French**, with heavy English use in the expat/EU world. Easy to live in English. See [French or Dutch](/blog/french-or-dutch-brussels-language).
- **Antwerp** is firmly **Dutch-speaking (Flemish)**. English is widely spoken, but daily life leans Dutch, and learning some Dutch matters more for integration.

If you already have (or want) French, Brussels is smoother. If you're keen on Dutch or plan to work in Flanders, Antwerp fits.

## Jobs & opportunity

**Brussels** dominates for international careers: EU institutions, NATO, hundreds of NGOs, lobbying, international law, and a growing tech scene. **Antwerp** is strong in logistics (Europe's second-biggest port), diamonds, fashion, chemicals, and creative industries. For most internationals arriving without Dutch, Brussels has the wider English-speaking job market.

## Cost of living & housing

Both are affordable by Western-European standards, with Antwerp often a touch cheaper on rent. Housing dynamics differ: Brussels has a deep, mature [coliving](/actors) and [colocation](/coliving-brussels-prices) market aimed at its transient international crowd, which makes landing solo genuinely easy (see our [rent prices guide](/blog/brussels-rent-prices-2026)). Antwerp has coliving too, but Brussels' scene is broader and more expat-oriented.

## Getting around & location

**Brussels** sits at the heart of Europe — Paris 1h20, Amsterdam 2h, London and Cologne close, and a central rail hub for [day trips](/blog/brussels-day-trips-guide). **Antwerp** is superbly connected too (30 min to Brussels) and very walkable/bikeable, but Brussels' central position is unmatched for weekend travel across Europe.

## So, which one?

For most **internationals arriving without Dutch, wanting the widest job market, an English-friendly life, and Europe on their doorstep** — Brussels is the natural choice, and its coliving scene makes the soft landing easy. If you're drawn to a **cooler, more design-led, Dutch-speaking city** and don't need the EU/international ecosystem, Antwerp is a wonderful place to live.

The good news? They're 30 minutes apart. Base yourself in one, and the other is a day trip away whenever you fancy a change.
