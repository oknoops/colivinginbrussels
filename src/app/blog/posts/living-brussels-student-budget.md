# How to Live in Brussels on a Student Budget

Brussels is one of the more affordable capital cities in Western Europe — but student and intern budgets are tight everywhere. With a few local tricks, you can live well here without draining your account. Here's how.

## Housing: your biggest lever

Rent is by far your largest cost, so this is where smart choices pay off most.

*   **Budget coliving** beats a lonely studio on value: one all-in payment (rent + utilities + wifi + cleaning) with no furniture to buy and instant community. The cheapest operators — [Ikoab](/actors/ikoab), [Colive](/actors/colive), and [Coloc Housing](/actors/coloc-housing) — start around €500–€650/month.
*   **Cheaper neighbourhoods:** [Schaerbeek](/neighborhoods/schaerbeek) and [Forest](/neighborhoods/forest) offer real value a tram ride from the centre.
*   **Compare before you sign:** our [price comparison](/coliving-brussels-prices) lays out every operator's range side by side.

See our full [student housing guide](/student-housing-brussels) for more.

## Transport: get the young rate

*   **STIB/MIVB** (metro/tram/bus) offers a heavily discounted annual pass for under-25s — a fraction of the adult price. If you're a student or young, this is a no-brainer.
*   **Trains (SNCB/NMBS):** the **Go Pass / Youth Multi** gives under-26s 10 trips at a low flat rate — perfect for cheap [day trips](/blog/brussels-day-trips-guide) to Ghent or the coast.
*   **Villo!** bike-share and walking are free-ish and Brussels is compact.

## Food: eat well for less

*   **Cook with housemates.** The single biggest food saving. Split a [Sunday market](/blog/brussels-markets-guide) shop at Gare du Midi and cook a group dinner — cheaper and more fun than eating alone.
*   **Lunch menus (*plat du jour*)** get you a proper restaurant meal at half the dinner price.
*   **Cheap eats:** a *mitraillette* or fries from a *fritkot*, a Turkish grill on Rue de Brabant, or African food in Matonge — all filling and under €10. See [where locals eat](/blog/where-locals-eat-brussels).
*   **Supermarket tiers:** Aldi and Lidl for basics, Colruyt for value, Delhaize/Carrefour for more choice.

## Free & cheap fun

Brussels rewards the broke-but-curious:

*   **First Sunday of the month:** many museums are **free** (see our [museums guide](/blog/best-museums-brussels)). Build your culture calendar around it.
*   **Free festivals:** Brussels Jazz Weekend, Fête de la Musique, and the Christmas market cost nothing to enjoy — see the [events calendar](/blog/brussels-festivals-events-calendar).
*   **Parks:** the Bois de la Cambre, Parc du Cinquantenaire, and the Ixelles ponds are free all year.
*   **Apéro at home:** a house terrace beats a bar tab. Belgian supermarket beer is cheap and excellent.

## Student discounts & admin

*   Carry your **student card** — discounts apply at museums, cinemas, and some shops.
*   Register your **mutuelle** (health insurance) — it reimburses much of your doctor visits.
*   Look into student jobs (Belgium has a favourable student-work regime with reduced social contributions up to an annual hour cap) if your visa/status allows.

## A realistic monthly budget

A rough student/intern month in Brussels might look like:

| Item | Approx. monthly |
| :--- | :--- |
| Budget coliving room (all-in) | €550–€700 |
| Groceries (cooking mostly) | €200–€250 |
| Transport (under-25 pass) | ~€12–€50 |
| Phone/SIM | €10–€20 |
| Fun (going out, trips) | €100–€200 |
| **Total** | **~€900–€1,200** |

Trim the "fun" line in lean months and it goes lower. All-in coliving keeps the big number predictable — no surprise utility bills.

## The one move that matters most

Get the housing right. A well-chosen budget [coliving room](/actors) fixes your single biggest cost, kills surprise bills, and hands you a built-in social life so you're not spending money to fight loneliness. Everything else is small tweaks around that.
