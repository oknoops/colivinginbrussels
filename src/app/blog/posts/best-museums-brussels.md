# The Best Museums in Brussels (And Which Ones Are Worth Your Weekend)

Brussels punches absurdly above its weight for museums. It's a compact city, so you can see world-class collections without ever getting on a train — and many are free on the first Sunday of the month, a ritual worth building your calendar around. Here's the newcomer's map, from the essentials to the delightfully weird.

## The essentials

### Magritte Museum
The single most Brussels museum there is. René Magritte — bowler hats, floating apples, "Ceci n'est pas une pipe" — was a local, and this museum holds the largest collection of his work in the world. Surrealism isn't just an art movement here; it's practically the national temperament. Start here to understand the city's sense of humour.

### Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium
A sprawling complex on the Mont des Arts covering the **Old Masters** (Bruegel, Rubens, Van Dyck), the **Fin-de-Siècle Museum**, and more. Bruegel's peasant scenes and snowy landscapes alone justify a visit. Give it a half-day; it's bigger than it looks.

### Comics Art Museum (Centre Belge de la Bande Dessinée)
Belgium invented (or at least perfected) the comic strip — Tintin, the Smurfs, Lucky Luke, Spirou. Housed in a stunning Art Nouveau building by Victor Horta, the Comics Art Museum celebrates the "ninth art" as national heritage. Even if you've never read a *bande dessinée*, the building itself is worth it. Then go hunt the comic-strip murals painted across the city centre.

## For the design and architecture lovers

### Horta Museum (Saint-Gilles)
Victor Horta was the father of Art Nouveau, and his own house-and-studio in [Saint-Gilles](/neighborhoods/saint-gilles) is a UNESCO-listed jewel — swirling ironwork, stained glass, light pouring through every surface. Small, magical, and a perfect entry point to the Art Nouveau that defines whole Brussels neighbourhoods.

### Musée Art & History (Cinquantenaire)
An encyclopaedic collection from antiquity to Art Deco, set in the grand Cinquantenaire park. Pair it with **Autoworld** next door (one of Europe's great vintage car collections) for a full day in the [Etterbeek](/neighborhoods/etterbeek) end of the city.

## The wonderfully unexpected

### Train World (Schaerbeek)
Even people who don't care about trains leave Train World grinning. Beautifully staged historic locomotives in a restored station — it's part museum, part theatre. One of the city's most quietly loved attractions.

### MIMA (Millennium Iconoclast Museum of Art)
On the canal in Molenbeek, MIMA is the young, punchy contemporary museum — street art, pop culture, and a rooftop view over a rapidly changing part of town. It signals where Brussels' art scene is heading, not just where it's been.

### Belgian Beer World & the "smaller" obsessions
Housed in the old stock exchange (the Bourse), **Belgian Beer World** turns the national drink into an interactive experience, ending with a tasting on a rooftop terrace over the city centre. For the truly curious, Brussels also offers a **Sewer Museum**, a **Museum of Musical Instruments (MIM)** with a spectacular Art Nouveau facade and rooftop café, and the **Cantillon Brewery** — a working, family-run lambic brewery that's essentially a living museum of sour beer.

## What's new and changing

Brussels' museum landscape keeps shifting. The big long-term project everyone watches is **Kanal – Centre Pompidou**, the transformation of a former Citroën garage on the canal into a massive modern art museum in partnership with Paris's Pompidou — a signal of the city's cultural ambition. Check current opening status before you plan around it, as major phases open in stages. Meanwhile the **Design Museum Brussels** on the Heysel plateau keeps a strong rotating programme for anyone into 20th-century plastics, furniture, and graphic design.

## How to do museums like a resident, not a tourist

*   **First Sunday of the month:** many federal and city museums are **free**. Locals plan around it. Go early — everyone else has the same idea.
*   **The Brussels Card:** if you're doing a museum blitz (useful when friends visit), the card bundles entry and transport.
*   **Rainy-day strategy:** Brussels weather does you a favour here. Keep a mental shortlist of two or three museums for grey afternoons, and the climate stops being an enemy.
*   **Go with housemates:** museum trips are a low-pressure way to bond with the people you live with. Many [coliving houses](/actors) organise cultural outings, and first-Sunday-free days are the natural excuse.

## The one to start with

If you only do one in your first month, make it the **Magritte Museum** followed by a wander down to the Comics Art Museum. Together they explain more about the Belgian imagination — surreal, funny, a little melancholic — than any guidebook could.
