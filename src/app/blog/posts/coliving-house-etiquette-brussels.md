# Coliving House Etiquette: How to Be a Great Housemate in Brussels

Coliving gives you an instant community — but a great house isn't automatic. It's built by people who know the unwritten rules of shared living. Whether you're moving into a [Cohabs](/actors/cohabs) townhouse or a cozy [Ikoab](/actors/ikoab) share, here's how to be the housemate everyone's glad arrived.

## The golden rule: leave it better than you found it

Ninety percent of coliving harmony comes down to this. The shared kitchen, the bathroom, the living room — treat them like they belong to everyone, because they do.

*   **Clean as you cook.** Wash your pan, wipe the counter, don't leave a "I'll do it later" pile. Later never comes and resentment does.
*   **The dishwasher isn't a storage unit.** Empty it when it's done if you're passing.
*   **Bathroom after you = bathroom for the next person.** Squeegee the shower, no hair in the drain, replace the loo roll.

## Respect the shared fridge (a sacred space)

The fridge is where housemate relationships live and die:

*   **Label your shelf/items** if the house works that way.
*   **Don't eat what isn't yours** — the cardinal sin. If in doubt, ask.
*   **Bin your expired stuff.** Nobody wants to play "whose science experiment is this?"

## Sound, sleep, and shared rhythms

*   **Learn the house's quiet hours** and honour them — people have early meetings, exams, and stages.
*   **Take calls in your room**, not at the shared table (especially work/video calls).
*   **Headphones are a love language.** Use them in common areas.
*   **Heads-up for parties.** Hosting friends? Tell the house first, keep it reasonable, and clean up the same night.

## Do your part on the chores

Most [coliving houses](/actors) have professional cleaning of common areas, but the day-to-day is on residents:

*   If there's a **rota**, do your turn without being chased.
*   **Take the bins out** — and learn Belgium's strict sorting (blue PMC bags, coloured collection days). Getting it wrong means the whole house's bags get left on the pavement.
*   **Restock shared basics** (dish soap, sponges) when you use the last of them.

## The social side: show up, don't force it

The best houses balance togetherness and privacy:

*   **Say yes to house dinners and apéros**, especially early on. This is how a house of strangers becomes friends — and it's the whole point of coliving.
*   **But respect "quiet mode."** Someone reading with headphones in the corner isn't being antisocial; they're recharging. Don't take it personally.
*   **Introduce yourself to new arrivals.** You were new once. A five-minute welcome makes someone's whole week.
*   **Communicate kindly and directly.** Belgian-style: address the small thing (the dishes, the noise) early and politely, before it festers. A quick "hey, mind grabbing your pan later?" beats a passive-aggressive group-chat message.

## When friction happens (it will)

Living with people means occasional friction. Handle it like an adult:

1.  **Assume good intent.** Most annoyances are forgetfulness, not malice.
2.  **Talk directly and early**, one-to-one, not via the group chat pile-on.
3.  **Use the house manager.** Most operators have someone whose job is exactly this — [Sharies](/actors/sharies) and [Comoon](/actors/comoon), for instance, have on-site management. Escalate real issues to them.

## Why good etiquette pays you back

Here's the thing: the effort compounds. Be the housemate who cleans up, says yes to dinner, and welcomes newcomers, and you'll find yourself in the warm centre of the house — invited to everything, helped when you need it, and genuinely at home. That's the real product coliving sells, and you're a co-creator of it.
