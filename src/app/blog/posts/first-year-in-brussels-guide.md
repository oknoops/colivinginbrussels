# Your First Year in Brussels: What to Expect

Your first year in Brussels follows a rhythm that almost every newcomer recognises in hindsight: a frantic arrival, a slog of paperwork, a slow build of friendships, and then — somewhere around the seasons turning — the moment the city clicks and starts to feel like home. Knowing that arc in advance takes the sting out of the hard bits. Here's a realistic, month-by-month look at what to expect, so you can pace yourself and enjoy the ride.

## Months 1–2: Arrival and the soft landing

The first weeks are pure logistics. You'll be hunting for a place to live, learning the tram map, and discovering that shops close earlier than you expect. The smartest move here is housing that removes friction: **coliving** gives you a furnished, move-in-ready room, all-in bills, and — most valuably — instant housemates when you know no one.

## Months 2–3: The admin phase

This is the least glamorous stretch, so brace for it. The big one is **domiciliation** — registering your address at your local commune, required for any stay longer than three months. It underpins your residency, healthcare, and daily life, so start early and be patient; Belgian admin moves at its own pace.

You'll also sort a bank account, health insurance (mutuelle), and a local SIM. Exact steps depend on your nationality and situation, so always confirm current requirements on the **official commune and immigration websites** rather than relying on a friend's outdated experience. Keep a folder of documents — you'll be asked for them more than once.

## Months 3–6: Building community

By now the paperwork eases and the real settling begins. This is when Brussels stops being a checklist and becomes a life. The key is consistency: show up repeatedly and friendships form.

- **Lean on your coliving.** Shared dinners and house events are the fastest route to a social circle.
- **Follow the city's calendar.** Check [what's on](/whats-on) for festivals, markets, and seasonal events, and just go.
- **Become a regular somewhere.** One café, one sports club, one language exchange — familiarity does the rest.

Many people hit a small wobble around month four, when the novelty fades but the roots aren't deep yet. It's normal, and it passes. Keep saying yes.

## Months 6–12: The seasons and the click

Brussels reveals itself through its seasons. **Autumn** brings cosy café culture and the city's famous grey skies — invest in a good raincoat and an indoor hobby. **Winter** is quiet but magical around the holidays. **Spring** is when the city exhales, parks fill up, and terraces reopen. **Summer** is festival season and long light evenings.

Somewhere in this stretch, the click happens. You give directions to a lost tourist. You have a favourite frites stand. Your housemates become your friends. The admin is behind you, the seasons make sense, and the strange grey city you moved to has quietly become home.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to feel settled in Brussels?**
Most newcomers feel genuinely at home somewhere between months four and eight. The first months are heavy on logistics; community and comfort build steadily after that, especially if you live somewhere social like a coliving.

**When do I need to register my address?**
If you're staying longer than three months, domiciliation at your local commune is required. Start it early, as it underpins residency, healthcare, and other admin. Confirm current steps on the official commune website.

**What's the hardest part of the first year?**
Usually the admin phase and a brief morale dip around month four, when the novelty fades before your roots are deep. Both are normal and temporary — consistency with people and routines gets you through.
