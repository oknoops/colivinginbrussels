# Coliving vs Airbnb in Brussels: Which Is Better for a 1–6 Month Stay?

You're moving to Brussels for a few months and the instinct is to book an Airbnb. It's familiar, it's instant, and you don't have to think. But for anything longer than a couple of weeks, Airbnb quietly becomes the expensive, isolating choice. Here's the honest comparison.

## The cost reality

Airbnb pricing is built for short tourist stays, and even with monthly discounts it rarely competes with coliving on a per-month basis in Brussels.

| | Airbnb (monthly) | Coliving (all-in) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Typical cost | €1,200–€2,500+ | €500–€1,500 |
| Utilities & wifi | Usually included | Included |
| Cleaning | Sometimes, often a big one-off fee | Weekly common areas |
| Service/booking fees | 10–20% on top | None (one rent) |
| Furniture & kitchen | Yes | Yes |

For a 1–6 month stay, coliving is very often **half the price or less** — and you skip the Airbnb service fees and surprise cleaning charges. Compare real operator prices on our [price page](/coliving-brussels-prices).

## The lifestyle difference

Money aside, the bigger gap is *how it feels*:

*   **Airbnb = a room, alone.** Great for a week. Over months, it's isolating — you arrive to an empty flat in a new city and have to build a social life from zero.
*   **Coliving = a home with people.** You land into housemates, shared dinners, and a ready-made friend group. For newcomers, this is the difference between a lonely stint and the best few months of your life. See [is coliving right for you](/blog/is-coliving-right-for-you-brussels).

## The admin difference

*   **Registration (domiciliation):** Belgium requires you to register your address if staying over 3 months. Most [coliving operators](/actors) provide a proper rental contract that allows this. Airbnb hosts usually **won't** let you register — a real problem for longer stays.
*   **Flexibility:** coliving leases run 1–6 months with clear terms; Airbnb month-to-month works but at premium nightly-derived rates.
*   **Support:** coliving houses have managers; Airbnb support is remote and hit-or-miss.

## When Airbnb *is* the right call

To be fair, Airbnb wins when:
*   You're staying **under 2–3 weeks**.
*   You need something **tonight** while you sort longer-term housing.
*   You want a **whole private apartment** with total solitude and will pay for it.

A smart play many newcomers use: book a **few nights on Airbnb** to land, then move straight into a coliving room you lined up before arriving. Our [matchmaker](/matchmaker) helps you have that room ready.

## When coliving wins (most of the time)

For the typical newcomer — **staying 1–6 months, alone, wanting to actually meet people and not overspend** — coliving wins on cost, community, and admin. It's not close.

## The bottom line

Airbnb is a great hotel alternative and a terrible medium-term home. If you're in Brussels for a month or more, coliving is cheaper, warmer, and far less lonely — and it solves the registration headache Airbnb creates. Use Airbnb for your first few nights if you must, then move into a real home.
