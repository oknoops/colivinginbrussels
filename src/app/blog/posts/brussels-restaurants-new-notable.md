# New & Notable Restaurants in Brussels: Where to Eat Right Now

Brussels' food scene moves fast — new bistros, delis, coffee bars and gelaterias open constantly, and the best ones fill up quickly. Below is a newcomer-friendly shortlist of **recently opened and currently buzzing spots**, plus the timeless institutions worth knowing. We keep this curated and link to sources so you can check details and book — for the freshest openings, the local review site [Brussels Kitchen](https://www.brusselskitchen.com/en/) is the reference.

*(Openings and hours change — always check the venue directly before you go.)*

## Fresh on the radar

A few spots recently getting attention on the Brussels food scene:

- **Alimentation** — a deli-counter concept doing seasonal, produce-led plates. The kind of place that blurs grocer and restaurant.
- **Horta** — organic grocery meets seasonal cuisine, leaning fresh, green and local.
- **Cosmos** — a "soil & sea" kitchen with a vegetable-forward, gastronomic bent for a special dinner.
- **Le Restaurant du Chaudron** — a community-driven restaurant with a warm, local feel.
- **Gelateria Giotto** — for genuinely good Italian-style gelato when the sun's out.
- **Lil' Bouche** — a specialty-coffee café for a slower morning or a laptop hour.

These come and go with the seasons — see the full, current list on [Brussels Kitchen](https://www.brusselskitchen.com/en/).

## The local institutions (always a safe bet)

New is exciting, but these Brussels classics never disappoint:

- **Fish bars at Sainte-Catherine** — grey-shrimp croquettes and oysters standing at the counter with a glass of white. Peak Brussels. (More in [where locals eat](/blog/where-locals-eat-brussels).)
- **Maison Antoine** (Place Jourdan) & **Frit Flagey** (Place Flagey) — the two legendary *fritkots*.
- **Brown cafés / estaminets** for *stoemp*, *carbonnade* and *vol-au-vent* — Belgian comfort food done right.
- **Matonge** (Ixelles) for Congolese and West African food; **Rue de Brabant** (Schaerbeek) for Turkish and Middle Eastern grills — the city's best-value eating.

Our full guide to [Belgian foods to try](/blog/belgian-food-you-must-try) and [Brussels for food lovers](/blog/brussels-for-food-lovers) go deeper.

## Where to eat, by neighbourhood

- **[Ixelles](/neighborhoods/ixelles)** (Châtelain, Flagey) — the densest restaurant zone, from bistros to brunch.
- **[Saint-Gilles](/neighborhoods/saint-gilles)** (Parvis) — bohemian, natural wine, small plates.
- **[Brussels City](/neighborhoods/brussels-city)** (Sainte-Catherine, Dansaert, Saint-Géry) — seafood, trendy tables and weekend brunch.
- **[Schaerbeek](/neighborhoods/schaerbeek)** — multicultural and great value.

## How to eat well as a newcomer

1. **Lunch menus (*plat du jour*)** — the same kitchens at half the dinner price. The food-lover's insider move.
2. **Book ahead** for the buzzy new spots, especially Thursday–Saturday.
3. **Follow [Brussels Kitchen](https://www.brusselskitchen.com/en/)** for the latest openings, and [visit.brussels](https://www.visit.brussels/en) for food events.
4. **Eat with housemates.** In a [coliving house](/actors), trying the new place is more fun with a group — and someone always has a recommendation.

## The bottom line

Brussels rewards the curious eater: world-class classics, a fast-moving new-openings scene, and some of the best-value multicultural food in Europe. Keep this list for the institutions, watch Brussels Kitchen for what's new, and you'll always have somewhere good to eat.
