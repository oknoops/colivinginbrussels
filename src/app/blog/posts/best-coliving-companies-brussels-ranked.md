# The Best Coliving Companies in Brussels, Ranked by Who They Suit

Ask ten people for the **best coliving brand in Brussels** and you'll get ten different answers — because "best" depends entirely on what you want. A student on a tight budget, a designer who cares about interiors, and an EU trainee who needs a room near the institutions are all looking for different things. So instead of forcing a single ranking, we've grouped the city's twelve real operators by the kind of person each one suits best. Find your persona below.

## If you're on a budget

The cheapest coliving in Brussels still gives you a furnished room with all bills included, usually starting around **€500–€600 per month all-in**. That's often cheaper than renting an empty studio once you add furniture, utilities and wifi.

- **[Ikoab](/actors/ikoab)** — consistently among the most affordable, with rooms from around €500. A no-frills, practical choice for making the numbers work.
- **[Colive](/actors/colive)** — rooms from around €600, spread across several houses, good for budget-conscious newcomers who still want a proper community.
- **[Coloc Housing](/actors/coloc-housing)** — another value-first operator from around €600, well suited to first-timers.

See how these stack up against everything else on our [operators page](/actors) and the [coliving prices guide](/coliving-brussels-prices).

## If you want a social, community-first home

Some houses are built around shared dinners, events and an active group chat. If your priority is meeting people fast — the main reason many newcomers pick coliving — look at the more community-driven operators.

- **[Cohabs](/actors/cohabs)** — one of the most recognised names, known for beautifully renovated houses and an active member community across the city.
- **[Corners](/actors/corners)** — design-forward houses with a strong sense of shared living and communal spaces.
- **[Comoon](/actors/comoon)** — community-oriented houses where the social side is front and centre.

## If you care about premium design

Willing to pay more for interiors that feel like a boutique hotel? These operators lean into architecture, furniture and finish. Expect prices toward the upper half of the €500–€1,500 range.

- **[Cohabs](/actors/cohabs)** — polished, high-spec renovations with a consistent aesthetic.
- **[Corners](/actors/corners)** — strong on design and atmosphere.
- **[Habyt](/actors/habyt)** — a large international operator with a modern, standardised look and professional management.

## If you want privacy within a shared home

Not everyone wants a party house. If you'd rather have your own well-equipped space and keep communal contact light, these tend to offer larger private rooms or studio-style units.

- **[Morton Place](/actors/morton-place)** — geared toward residents who want comfort and a calmer atmosphere.
- **[Neybor](/actors/neybor)** — a balanced option for people who value both their own space and a friendly house.
- **[Live Colonies](/actors/livecolonies)** — houses with a homely feel for those who prefer things low-key.

## If you're near the EU quarter

Trainees, consultants and EU-institution staff often want to be within easy reach of Schuman and the European district. [Etterbeek](/coliving/etterbeek) and [Ixelles](/coliving/ixelles) are the classic bases, with easy metro links to the institutions.

- **[Sharies](/actors/sharies)** — practical shared living aimed at working newcomers.
- **[Co-Homing](/actors/co-homing)** — flexible houses that suit professionals moving to the city for a fixed term.

Prefer to choose by area rather than brand? Compare districts on our [neighbourhoods guide](/neighborhoods).

## What they all have in common

Whichever persona you fit, Brussels coliving shares a few reassuring basics:

1. **All-in pricing** — rent, utilities, wifi and communal cleaning bundled into one monthly figure.
2. **Flexible leases** — commonly 3–6 months, far shorter than a standard Belgian lease.
3. **No Belgian guarantor** — most operators don't require one, removing a major barrier for new arrivals.
4. **Domiciliation** — for stays over three months you'll register your address at the commune; check the operator can support this before signing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which coliving company in Brussels is the cheapest?**
Ikoab (from around €500), Colive (from around €600) and Coloc Housing (from around €600) are typically the most affordable, though the exact rate depends on the room and house.

**Do these operators require a deposit?**
Most ask for a deposit, but the amounts and terms vary. What they usually don't require is a Belgian guarantor, which is the bigger hurdle for newcomers.

**Can I switch houses within the same operator?**
Some larger operators like Habyt and Cohabs let members move between houses or even cities, which is handy if your plans change. Confirm the policy directly with the operator.
