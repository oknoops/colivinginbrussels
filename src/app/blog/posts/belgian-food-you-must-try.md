# 13 Belgian Foods You Have to Try When You Move to Brussels

Belgium takes food seriously — arguably more seriously than any country its size. Beyond the famous exports (waffles, chocolate, beer, fries) lies a whole world of comfort dishes, street food, and regional treats. Here's your first-month eating bucket list, one delicious item at a time.

## The savoury essentials

### 1. Frites (twice-fried, from a fritkot)
The national obsession, and nothing like the limp "French" fry. Twice-fried, crisp outside and fluffy inside, served in a paper cone from a *fritkot* with a sauce (andalouse, samouraï, or classic mayo). Eaten standing up. Non-negotiable. Try **Maison Antoine** or **Frit Flagey**.

### 2. Mitraillette
The student/night-out legend: a baguette loaded with fried meat (or a burger), topped with frites and drowned in sauce. Enormous, cheap, and indefensible — a rite of passage after a night out.

### 3. Stoemp
Pure Brussels comfort. Mashed potato blended with vegetables (carrot, leek, or cabbage), usually served with a sausage or bacon. Brown-café winter food at its coziest.

### 4. Carbonnade Flamande (Stoofvlees)
Beef slow-braised in Belgian brown beer until meltingly tender, slightly sweet, deeply savoury. The national stew, ideally eaten on a grey day with — of course — frites.

### 5. Moules-frites
Mussels steamed in white wine or beer, served with (naturally) frites. A Belgian classic, best in season (months with an "r"). Skip the tourist strip of Rue des Bouchers; find a neighbourhood spot or cook them at home — they're cheap and easy.

### 6. Croquettes aux crevettes grises
Grey shrimp croquettes — creamy, crumbed, fried, and a genuine delicacy. Eat them standing at a fish bar around **Place Sainte-Catherine** with a glass of white wine for the full Brussels experience.

### 7. Vol-au-vent
Puff pastry filled with chicken, meatballs, and mushrooms in a creamy sauce. Belgian Sunday-lunch nostalgia on a plate, usually with — you guessed it — frites.

### 8. Boulets (à la Liégeoise)
Meatballs in a rich sweet-and-sour sauce made with Liège syrup (*sirop de Liège*). A regional star that's spread everywhere. Comforting and distinctly Belgian.

## The sweet legends

### 9. Waffles — Brussels vs Liège
Two very different beasts. The **Brussels waffle** is light, airy, rectangular, dusted with icing sugar. The **Liège waffle** is dense, chewy, and caramelised with pearl sugar, eaten warm and plain from a stand. Locals eat them *plain* — the mountains of Nutella and strawberries are a tourist thing.

### 10. Belgian chocolate
A national art form. Beyond the big names, seek out neighbourhood *chocolatiers* and pralines made fresh. A box of good pralines is the correct gift for any Belgian host — and an excellent reason to make friends with a *chocolatier*.

### 11. Speculoos
The spiced, caramelised cinnamon biscuit that flavours half the country — served with coffee, crushed into desserts, and spread from a jar (*pâte de speculoos*) onto bread. Dangerously good.

### 12. Cuberdon (Neus / "little nose")
A cone-shaped purple sweet with a firm sugar shell and a soft, gooey raspberry-flavoured centre. Sold from market stalls (there's a famous rivalry between Ghent's cuberdon vendors). Weird, sticky, and wonderful — a proper Belgian curiosity.

### 13. Tarte au riz / Belgian pastries
Belgian bakeries (*boulangeries/pâtisseries*) are a daily pleasure. Try the **tarte au riz** (rice tart, a Verviers speciality), a *couque*, or a *cramique* (raisin brioche). Buying fresh bread and a pastry from your local bakery is one of the small rituals that makes you feel at home.

## How to eat your way through the list

*   **Markets** are your friend — the [Sunday market at Gare du Midi](/blog/brussels-markets-guide) and Flagey are treasure troves of cheap Belgian eats.
*   **Brown cafés (*estaminets*)** serve the comfort classics (stoemp, carbonnade, vol-au-vent) without pretension.
*   **Cook the classics at home.** Moules-frites, stoemp, and carbonnade are surprisingly easy and cheap to make. In a [coliving house](/actors), a Belgian-themed house dinner is a brilliant way to try several dishes at once and get your Belgian housemates to show off their family recipes.

## Where to start

If you tackle just three this week: **frites from a real fritkot**, **shrimp croquettes at a fish bar**, and a **plain Liège waffle from a stand**. Do those, and you've eaten more like a Belgian than most people manage all year. Then work your way down the list — *smakelijk!* / *bon appétit!*
