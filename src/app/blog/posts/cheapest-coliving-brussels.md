# The Cheapest Coliving in Brussels: Best Budget Options in 2026

Coliving in Brussels doesn't have to be expensive. While premium operators run €1,000+, there's a whole tier of budget-friendly houses where an all-inclusive room starts around €500–€650 — bills, wifi, and community included. Here's where to find the best value.

## The most budget-friendly operators

### [Ikoab](/actors/ikoab) — from ~€500
One of the pioneers of Belgian coliving and consistently among the cheapest. Classic "Auberge Espagnole" house shares with real community energy, aimed squarely at students and young professionals watching their budget.

### [Colive](/actors/colive) — from ~€600
A city-wide network with a truly all-inclusive model (even household insurance). Family-style houses, practical furnished rooms, and a great entry point for interns and first-timers.

### [Coloc Housing](/actors/coloc-housing) — from ~€600
Accessible, flatshare-style coliving with flexible stays and all-in rent. Fewer frills, friendlier prices — easy to move into for a semester or a first job.

### [Co-Homing](/actors/co-homing) — from ~€700
A step up in polish but still reasonable, with a dozen central houses (many rooms with private bathrooms). Good value for the location.

Compare them all side by side on our [price page](/coliving-brussels-prices).

## The cheapest neighborhoods

Where you look matters as much as which operator:

*   **[Schaerbeek](/neighborhoods/schaerbeek)** — best value in the city, diverse and up-and-coming, with good tram links (€600–€950).
*   **[Forest](/neighborhoods/forest)** — residential, affordable, and right next to trendy Saint-Gilles (€650–€1,000).
*   **[Saint-Gilles](/neighborhoods/saint-gilles)** — bohemian and central, cheaper than neighbouring Ixelles (€700–€1,100).

Avoid defaulting to pricey Ixelles or Uccle if budget is tight — one tram stop over can save you hundreds. See our [neighborhood comparison](/blog/where-to-live-brussels-neighborhood-comparison).

## Why "cheap" coliving still beats a cheap studio

A €600 coliving room often beats a €650 studio once you count what's included:
*   No utility bills (water, gas, electricity)
*   No separate wifi contract
*   No furniture to buy
*   No agency fee
*   Weekly cleaning of common areas
*   A built-in social life (priceless when you're new)

A bare studio at the same headline price leaves you paying €150–€200 more in bills and setup — and eating alone. Read our full [budget guide](/blog/living-brussels-student-budget).

## How to save even more

1.  **Shared rooms or larger houses** sometimes have the lowest per-person rents.
2.  **Move outside peak season** (avoid Sep/Feb student rushes) for better availability and deals.
3.  **Longer commitments** occasionally unlock lower monthly rates — ask.
4.  **Look for move-in offers.** Operators sometimes run first-month discounts.
5.  **Cook with housemates** — the biggest ongoing saving after rent.

## A realistic cheap-but-good setup

Budget operator (Ikoab/Colive/Coloc Housing) + a value neighbourhood (Schaerbeek/Forest) + cooking with housemates = an all-in Brussels life for roughly **€700–€900/month**, community included. That's hard to beat anywhere in Western Europe's capitals.
