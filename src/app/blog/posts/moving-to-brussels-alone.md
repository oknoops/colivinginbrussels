# Moving to Brussels Alone: How to Land & Build a Life

Moving to Brussels alone can feel daunting — new city, new language(s), no ready-made friend group. But here's the reassuring truth: Brussels is one of the easiest cities in Europe to arrive in solo. It's small enough to feel human, international enough that being a newcomer is normal, and set up so that you can build a social life within weeks. This guide walks you through landing softly, meeting people, and getting the admin sorted so you can actually enjoy the city.

## Start with housing that comes with people

The single biggest lever for settling fast when you move alone is **where you live**. A flat you found on your own can be lonely for the first few months. Coliving flips that: you get a private room plus shared common spaces and, crucially, housemates who are often newcomers too.

Coliving in Brussels usually runs **all-in from around €500 to €1,500 per month** — rent, utilities, wifi, and often cleaning and community events bundled into one payment. Leases tend to be flexible, commonly **3 to 6 months**, which is ideal when you're not yet sure which neighbourhood you'll love. And because coliving operators handle the contract directly, you typically **don't need a Belgian guarantor**, which removes a classic barrier for people who've just arrived.

## Pick a neighbourhood that matches your energy

Where you land shapes how quickly you feel at home. A few solo-friendly options:

- **[Ixelles](/neighborhoods/ixelles)** — buzzy, international, and packed with cafés and bars; great if you want to meet people easily.
- **[Saint-Gilles](/neighborhoods/saint-gilles)** — creative, walkable, and full of character, a favourite for a younger crowd.
- **[Schaerbeek](/neighborhoods/schaerbeek)** and **[Forest](/neighborhoods/forest)** — better value while still lively, worth a look if budget matters.

Compare them all on the [neighbourhoods page](/neighborhoods) before you decide.

## The admin, without the overwhelm

Brussels admin has a reputation for being slow, but if you tackle it in order it's manageable:

- **Domiciliation** — if you're staying longer than three months, you register your address at your local commune. This underpins almost everything else, so start it early.
- **Residency and paperwork** — the exact steps depend on your nationality and situation. Always confirm current requirements on the **official commune and immigration websites**, as rules change and vary case by case.
- **Bank, mutuelle (health insurance), and a local SIM** — practical basics you'll sort in the first weeks.

Coliving helps here too: many operators are used to newcomers and can point you to the right commune office and paperwork, so you're not decoding it alone.

## Build your social life in the first month

Settling isn't just logistics — it's people. A few reliable ways to meet them:

1. **Say yes to your coliving's community events.** Shared dinners and house drinks are the fastest friendships you'll make.
2. **Follow the city's calendar.** Check [what's on](/whats-on) for markets, festivals, and events, and just show up.
3. **Use your neighbourhood.** Become a regular at one café; familiarity turns strangers into friends.
4. **Join interest-based meetups** — sport, language exchange, coworking — where being new is the norm.

Give it a month of consistent effort and Brussels stops feeling like a foreign city and starts feeling like yours.

## FAQ

**Do I need a Belgian guarantor to rent coliving?**
Usually not. Most coliving operators contract with you directly and don't require a Belgian guarantor, which makes them especially convenient when you've just arrived and don't yet have local ties.

**How quickly can I move into a coliving?**
Often quite fast — coliving rooms are furnished and move-in ready, and leases are typically flexible (3 to 6 months). Availability varies by operator, so check listings and enquire early.

**Is Brussels a good city to move to alone?**
Yes. It's compact, highly international, and used to newcomers, so building a social life is realistic within weeks — especially if you choose housing, like coliving, that comes with a built-in community.
