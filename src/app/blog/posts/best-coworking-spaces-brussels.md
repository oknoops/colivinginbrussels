# The Best Coworking Spaces in Brussels for Remote Workers

Brussels is a quietly excellent base for remote workers: central in Europe, affordable, and well-supplied with coworking spaces of every flavour — from polished corporate hubs to scrappy creative studios. Here's how to choose one, plus a tip most guides miss (the coliving houses that fold coworking right into your home).

## What to look for in a coworking space

Before you commit to a membership, weigh:
- **Location & commute** — near your [neighbourhood](/neighborhoods) or on a good tram line.
- **Membership flexibility** — day passes, part-time, or full hot-desk/dedicated-desk options.
- **The essentials** — fast reliable wifi, plenty of sockets, and quiet booths for calls.
- **Community & events** — some spaces run networking, talks, and socials, which matters a lot when you're new in town.
- **Meeting rooms** — if you take client calls or host, check availability and cost.

## The types of coworking in Brussels

Brussels has a deep coworking scene spanning several categories:

- **Large professional networks** — polished spaces with multiple locations across the city, meeting rooms, and business-grade everything. Best for consultants and anyone client-facing.
- **Creative & independent studios** — smaller, characterful spaces (often in [Saint-Gilles](/neighborhoods/saint-gilles), [Ixelles](/neighborhoods/ixelles), and the canal area) with a freelance, design-y crowd. Best for creatives and a more personal vibe.
- **Café-coworking hybrids** — pay-per-use spots that blur into the café scene. Good for lighter days; see our [cafés to work from](/blog/best-cafes-to-work-from-brussels) guide.
- **Niche & sector hubs** — spaces focused on tech, social impact, or specific industries, great for meeting people in your field.

The scene clusters around the [city centre](/neighborhoods/brussels-city)/canal, the [EU quarter](/eu-quarter-housing), and the trendy southern communes — pick by where you live and who you want to be around.

## How to try before you commit

1. **Buy a day pass** at two or three spaces before choosing a membership. The *feel* varies enormously.
2. **Visit mid-week, mid-morning** — the realistic working atmosphere.
3. **Test the wifi and a call booth** during your trial.
4. **Ask about the community** — events, member mix, and whether people actually talk to each other.

## The tip most guides miss: coworking at home

Here's the move for remote workers who also want to *not be isolated*: several Brussels [coliving operators](/actors) build coworking-grade workspaces into the house itself.

- **[Comoon](/actors/comoon)** includes a coworking space with meeting rooms.
- **[Neybor](/actors/neybor)** designs dedicated workspaces into its houses.
- **[Live Colonies](/actors/livecolonies)** and **[Cohabs](/actors/cohabs)** offer coworking floors and quiet zones.

The payoff: a real desk and fast wifi a staircase from your bed, *plus* housemates around you so remote work doesn't tip into loneliness — and you save the coworking membership fee. You can still grab a day pass elsewhere when you want a change of scene. We dig into this in [working remotely from a coliving](/blog/working-remotely-brussels-coliving).

## The remote worker's Brussels setup

Deep-focus mornings at your coliving workspace or a coworking hot-desk, lighter afternoons at a [specialty-coffee café](/blog/best-coffee-shops-brussels), and after-work apéros on a terrace with the people you live and work near. Affordable, central, and social — Brussels does remote work very well.
