# The Best Day Trips from Brussels (All Under 90 Minutes)

One of the quiet joys of living in Brussels: the whole of Belgium — and half of Western Europe — is on your doorstep. The country is small, the trains are frequent and cheap, and a spontaneous Saturday can take you to a medieval canal city or the North Sea. Here's your escape map.

## The classic Belgian cities

### Ghent (~30 min by train)
The local favourite — a stunning medieval city with canals, a castle (Gravensteen), and a young, studenty energy that Bruges lacks. Less touristy than Bruges, more lived-in. Grab a *cuberdon* from a street stall and walk the Graslei waterfront. Arguably the best all-round day trip.

### Bruges (~60 min)
The postcard. Cobbled streets, canals, chocolate shops, and the famous Belfry. Yes, it's touristy — go early, wander beyond the main square, and it's genuinely magical. Perfect for when friends and family visit.

### Antwerp (~45 min)
Belgium's cool, cosmopolitan second city — fashion, diamonds, a spectacular railway station (one of the world's most beautiful), a gritty-chic port vibe, and great nightlife. The most "big city" of the day trips.

### Leuven (~25 min)
A compact, gorgeous university town with a jaw-dropping town hall and Belgium's oldest university. Home of Stella Artois, and a great low-key half-day.

### Mechelen (~25 min)
Underrated, charming, and quiet — a lovely smaller city between Brussels and Antwerp for when you want beauty without crowds.

## Nature & the coast

### The Belgian coast (~70–90 min)
Hop a train to **Ostend**, **Knokke**, or **Blankenberge** for North Sea air, beach, and *croquettes aux crevettes*. The coastal tram (Kusttram) runs the entire coastline if you want to hop between towns. Bracing even in summer — very Belgian.

### The Ardennes (~90 min+)
For forests, castles, kayaking, and hiking, the green, hilly Ardennes (Namur, Dinant, La Roche) are the outdoor escape. Dinant, with its cliffside citadel and the birthplace of the saxophone, makes a scenic day.

### Hallerbos "Blue Forest" (~40 min, spring only)
In late April/early May, the forest near Halle carpets in bluebells — a magical, brief, and very local spring pilgrimage.

## Cross the border

Brussels' central position means other countries are close:

*   **Lille, France (~35 min)** by high-speed train — a lovely French city for lunch and shopping.
*   **Amsterdam (~2 hrs)**, **Paris (~1h20)**, **Cologne (~1h50)**, **Luxembourg (~2h30)** — all doable, some as (long) day trips, all easy weekends. Compare Brussels and Amsterdam in our [expat comparison](/blog/brussels-vs-amsterdam-expat-comparison).

## How to do it cheaply

*   **SNCB/NMBS rail deals:** look at weekend tickets, the **Youth Multi/Go Pass** if you're under 26 (10 trips at a low flat rate), and discovery/standard tickets. Belgian domestic rail is remarkably affordable.
*   **Book nothing in advance** for domestic trips — just turn up; trains are frequent.
*   **Go with housemates.** Day trips are the best low-cost group activity, and splitting the discovery with people you live with turns a train ride into a proper adventure. Many [coliving houses](/actors) organise weekend outings — an easy way to see Belgium and bond at once.

## The newcomer's first three

If you do just three in your first months: **Ghent** (for the perfect all-rounder), **Antwerp** (for the city energy), and **the coast** (for a proper change of scenery). Belgium is small — see it while it's this easy.
