# Best Neighbourhoods in Brussels for Expats (2026)

Choosing among the **best neighbourhoods in Brussels for expats** is the single biggest decision you'll make when you move here. Brussels is a small, dense city of 19 communes, and each has a completely different feel — from leafy family suburbs to buzzing bar districts. This guide ranks the six most expat-friendly areas, explains who each one suits, and points you to coliving options so you can settle in without months of admin.

## What makes a neighbourhood "expat-friendly"?

Before the ranking, three things tend to matter most to newcomers:

- **Language mix** — areas where English, French and other languages coexist feel easier in your first months.
- **Commute** — proximity to the EU quarter, universities or the metro network.
- **Community** — cafés, markets and shared housing where it's easy to meet people.

## 1. Ixelles — the all-rounder

Ixelles (Elsene) is the default choice for a reason. It blends the student energy around the universities, the trendy Châtelain and Flagey squares, and a genuinely international crowd. You'll hear five languages on one café terrace. Rents are mid-to-high, but the variety of housing is huge.

**Best for:** first-time expats, young professionals, anyone who wants everything on their doorstep. Explore [coliving in Ixelles](/coliving/ixelles).

## 2. Etterbeek — closest to the EU bubble

If you work at the European institutions, Etterbeek is hard to beat. It sits right next to the EU quarter, so many people walk or cycle to work. It's calmer than Ixelles but still well connected, with good value for money.

**Best for:** EU staff, interns and anyone prioritising a short commute. See [coliving in Etterbeek](/coliving/etterbeek).

## 3. Saint-Gilles — creative and characterful

Saint-Gilles (Sint-Gillis) is the artsy, slightly bohemian option. Beautiful Art Nouveau facades, independent cafés, a real neighbourhood feel, and a diverse, creative population. It's popular with freelancers and people in design, media and the arts.

**Best for:** creatives, remote workers and social butterflies. Browse [coliving in Saint-Gilles](/coliving/saint-gilles).

## 4. Uccle — green and family-friendly

Uccle (Ukkel) is Brussels' leafy south — quieter, more residential, with parks, good schools and larger homes. It's a favourite of families and people who want space and calm over nightlife, while still being a reasonable ride from the centre.

**Best for:** families, senior professionals and anyone craving green space. See [coliving in Uccle](/coliving/uccle).

## 5. Woluwe-Saint-Lambert — suburban comfort

Woluwe-Saint-Lambert offers a polished, suburban lifestyle in the east: wide avenues, parks, shopping and an easy metro line into town. It's popular with international families and professionals who want comfort and safety.

**Best for:** families and professionals who prefer a quieter base. Explore [coliving in Woluwe-Saint-Lambert](/coliving/woluwe-saint-lambert).

## 6. Schaerbeek — up-and-coming value

Schaerbeek (Schaarbeek) is large, diverse and increasingly popular with younger expats priced out of the centre. It has grand townhouses, a strong multicultural character and improving connections. You get more space for your money here.

**Best for:** budget-conscious newcomers and creatives. See [coliving in Schaerbeek](/coliving/schaerbeek).

## FAQ

**Which Brussels neighbourhood is best for someone working at the EU?**
Etterbeek and the EU quarter are the closest, with Ixelles a strong runner-up for walkability and social life.

**Where should families with children look?**
Uccle and Woluwe-Saint-Lambert offer green space, schools and larger homes, while staying connected to the centre.

**Do I need to speak French to live in these areas?**
No. Brussels is officially bilingual (French/Dutch) and highly international. You'll manage in English in most expat-heavy areas, though learning some French helps with admin.
