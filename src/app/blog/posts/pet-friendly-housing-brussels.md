# Pet-Friendly Housing & Coliving in Brussels: What to Know

Relocating with a pet adds a real constraint to the housing hunt — and it's better to know the honest picture upfront. Here's the straight talk on finding pet-friendly coliving and rentals in Brussels, and how to give yourself the best shot.

## The honest reality for coliving

Most coliving houses in Brussels are **not pet-friendly by default**. Shared spaces, allergies, and housemate comfort make operators cautious, so a blanket "no pets" is common. But it's not a flat no everywhere:

*   **Case-by-case exceptions** happen, particularly for **small, quiet pets** (a calm cat more than a large dog).
*   **Individual houses vary** — one house under an operator may allow a cat while another won't. Always ask about the *specific* property.
*   **Larger dogs** are the hardest to place in shared living and may point you toward a private rental instead.

The takeaway: don't assume, but don't lose hope — ask directly and early. Browse [operators](/actors) and enquire specifically.

## Traditional rentals: more flexible, but check the lease

Private rentals are generally more pet-friendly than coliving, but:
*   Belgian landlords **can** set pet clauses in the lease — read it carefully.
*   A furnished place may be stricter than an unfurnished one.
*   Having your pet's paperwork (vaccination records, EU pet passport) and offering a slightly higher deposit can reassure a hesitant landlord.

See how leases work in our [renting in Brussels guide](/blog/renting-in-brussels-guide).

## What to ask before you commit

When enquiring anywhere, be upfront and specific:
1.  **"Is *this specific room/house* pet-friendly?"** (not the operator in general)
2.  **Any size, breed, or number limits?**
3.  **Extra deposit or monthly fee** for a pet?
4.  **Access to outdoor space** (garden/terrace) — important for dogs.
5.  **Housemate agreement** — in coliving, do current residents consent?

Being transparent from the first message saves everyone time and builds trust.

## Bringing a pet to Belgium: the basics

*   Pets from within the EU need an **EU pet passport**, microchip, and up-to-date rabies vaccination.
*   Non-EU arrivals face additional requirements — check current Belgian/EU import rules well before you travel.
*   Register with a **local vet** once you arrive; Brussels has plenty.

*(Rules change — always confirm current requirements with official Belgian/EU sources before moving.)*

## Brussels is a good city for pets

The housing hunt is the hard part; daily life is easy. Brussels is green and walkable, with big parks like the **Bois de la Cambre**, **Forêt de Soignes**, and **Parc du Cinquantenaire** for dog walks, plus plenty of pet-friendly café terraces. Once you're settled, your companion will do well here.

## Realistic strategy

1.  **Widen the net:** enquire with several [operators](/actors) *and* look at private rentals in parallel.
2.  **Lead with a calm, well-documented pet** and offer reassurance (deposit, references).
3.  **Prioritise houses with gardens/terraces** and ground-floor access for dogs.
4.  **Be patient and start early** — pet-friendly options are fewer, so give yourself runway.

## The bottom line

Pet-friendly coliving in Brussels exists but is limited — expect to ask around, favour small pets, and consider a private rental for larger dogs. Be transparent, come prepared with paperwork, and give yourself extra time. Once you're in, Brussels is a genuinely pet-happy city.
