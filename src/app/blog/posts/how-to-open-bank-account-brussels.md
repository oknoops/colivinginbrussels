# How to Open a Bank Account in Brussels as a Newcomer

You'll need a bank account fast in Brussels — for rent, your salary or stipend, and everyday life. The good news: you have options, and you don't have to wait for all your paperwork before you can transact. Here's the practical guide.

*(This is general orientation, not financial advice — banks' exact requirements change, so confirm with the bank directly.)*

## Two routes: digital first, local later

The smart newcomer approach is usually **both**:

### 1. A digital/EU account — instant, day one
Services like **Revolut**, **N26**, and **Wise** let you open a euro account with an IBAN in minutes from your phone, often before you've registered anywhere. Great for:
*   Receiving and sending money immediately
*   Paying your coliving deposit and first rent
*   Everyday spending while your local account is set up

For many interns and short-stayers, a digital account alone covers the whole stay.

### 2. A traditional Belgian bank — for the long haul
For a longer stay, a salaried job, or anything requiring a Belgian IBAN and local services, you'll want an account with a Belgian bank. The main players:
*   **KBC / KBC Brussels**
*   **BNP Paribas Fortis**
*   **ING Belgium**
*   **Belfius**

Some have English-language onboarding and expat-friendly branches; a few offer app-based opening. Belgian banks typically use the **Bancontact** system (the local debit standard accepted everywhere), which is genuinely useful to have.

## What documents you'll typically need

For a Belgian bank account, be ready with:
*   **Passport / national ID**
*   **Proof of address in Belgium** — your coliving rental agreement helps here
*   Often, proof of **registration (domiciliation)** at your commune, or at least that it's underway
*   Sometimes a **Belgian national number (rijksregisternummer)**, which you receive through registration

This is why the order matters: **register at your commune first**, and the bank step becomes far smoother. See our [registration guide](/blog/how-to-register-brussels-expat-guide) and [first-week guide](/blog/moving-to-brussels-first-week-guide).

## The recommended sequence

1.  **Day 1:** open a digital account (Revolut/N26/Wise) so you can pay your deposit and rent immediately.
2.  **Days 2–8:** register at your commune (domiciliation).
3.  **After registration:** open a Belgian bank account with proof of address + national number for salary, Bancontact, and long-term needs.

## Tips to avoid headaches

*   **Ask about English service** upfront — some branches and neobank-style Belgian offerings are much easier for non-French/Dutch speakers.
*   **Keep your coliving contract handy** — it's the proof-of-address document that unlocks most steps. This is another quiet advantage of [coliving](/actors): you get a clean, registerable rental agreement from day one.
*   **Watch monthly fees.** Some Belgian accounts have small monthly charges; digital banks are often free at the basic tier. Compare before committing.
*   **Get Bancontact.** Once you have a Belgian card, you'll understand why locals rely on it — it's accepted where foreign cards sometimes aren't.

## The bottom line

Open a digital account on day one so money moves immediately, register at your commune, then set up a Belgian bank account for the long term. Do it in that order and banking becomes one of the *easier* parts of settling into Brussels — leaving you free to focus on the fun stuff, like finding your favourite *fritkot*.
