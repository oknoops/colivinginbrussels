# Coliving vs Student Residence in Brussels: Which Is Better?

If you're a student heading to Brussels, one of the first big decisions is where to live — and increasingly the choice comes down to coliving vs student residence. Both give you a furnished room and a community of people your age, but they differ in cost, atmosphere, flexibility, and the admin involved. This guide breaks down the trade-offs so you can pick the option that actually fits your year, your budget, and your study life.

## The quick comparison

| Factor | Coliving | Student residence (kot) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Typical cost** | All-in ~€500–€1,500/mo (rent, bills, wifi, often events) | Often cheaper per month, but bills and extras may be separate |
| **Who lives there** | Mixed — students, young professionals, remote workers | Mostly students |
| **Community** | Organised events, shared common spaces, social by design | Social, but more informal and varies by building |
| **Flexibility** | Flexible leases, commonly 3–6 months | Often tied to the academic year |
| **Furnishing & bills** | Furnished, all-inclusive, move-in ready | Furnished, but bills/services vary |
| **Guarantor** | Usually no Belgian guarantor needed | May require a guarantor |
| **Admin** | Operator handles the contract; simple | Varies by provider; can be more paperwork |

## When a student residence (kot) wins

The classic Belgian **kot** — a student room, often in a shared house or dedicated residence — is a great fit if:

- **Budget is your top priority.** Base rents can be lower than coliving, especially in student-heavy areas.
- **You want an all-student environment.** If you'd rather live only among fellow students, a kot delivers exactly that.
- **You're staying the full academic year.** Kot contracts often align neatly with the school calendar.

The trade-offs: bills and extras aren't always included, the social scene depends heavily on who happens to live in your building, and some providers ask for a guarantor.

## When coliving wins

**Coliving** is the stronger pick if:

- **You value ready-made community.** Coliving is designed to be social, with organised events and shared common spaces — no waiting to see if your housemates click.
- **You want simplicity.** One all-in payment covers rent, utilities, and wifi, so there are no surprise bills to manage.
- **You need flexibility.** Leases of 3 to 6 months suit exchange semesters, internships, or a first year where your plans might shift.
- **You'd rather skip the guarantor.** Coliving operators typically contract directly and don't require a Belgian guarantor — a real advantage for international students.

The trade-off is price: the all-inclusive convenience of coliving usually costs a bit more than a bare kot. But once you add bills, wifi, and the value of a built-in community, the gap narrows.

## How to actually decide

If you're still torn, run through these questions honestly:

- **What's my true monthly ceiling?** Add up rent plus bills for each option, not just the headline rent, so you compare like with like.
- **How social do I want home to be?** If you want structured community and easy friendships, coliving is built for it. If you'd rather keep home low-key and find your people on campus, a kot may suit you.
- **How long am I staying?** A single semester or internship points toward coliving's flexible leases; a full three-year degree may suit a longer, cheaper kot arrangement.
- **Can I arrange a guarantor?** If not, coliving's no-guarantor norm removes a real obstacle, especially for international students arriving without local ties.

There's no universally "better" answer — the right choice depends on your budget, personality, and timeline. Both options give you a furnished room and people your own age; the difference is how much convenience and community you're paying for, and how long you're locked in. Sketch out the real all-in cost of two or three specific rooms before you sign anything, and the decision usually makes itself.

## FAQ

**Is coliving more expensive than a student residence?**
Base rent in a kot is often lower, but coliving bundles utilities, wifi, and community events into one all-in price (roughly €500–€1,500/mo). Once you add up separate bills for a kot, the real-world gap is usually smaller than it first appears.

**Do students need a guarantor for coliving in Brussels?**
Usually not. Most coliving operators contract with you directly and don't require a Belgian guarantor, which is a big plus for international students. Some traditional kots do ask for one.

**Which is better for an exchange semester?**
Coliving, in most cases — its flexible 3-to-6-month leases and instant community suit a short stay better than an academic-year kot contract. Remember that stays over three months require domiciliation at the commune.
