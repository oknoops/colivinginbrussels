# Erasmus in Brussels: How to Find Accommodation (Without the Stress)

Brussels is one of the best Erasmus destinations in Europe — central, international, affordable, and packed with other exchange students. But accommodation stresses everyone out. Here's how to land a room fast, on a student budget, with a built-in social life from day one.

## Your options as an Erasmus student

1.  **University housing / kot** — Belgian student rooms ("kots") through your host university (ULB, VUB, Saint-Louis, etc.). Cheap when available, but demand outstrips supply and you may be far from the action.
2.  **Private student residences** — purpose-built student housing; convenient but can be pricey and impersonal.
3.  **Coliving** — a private room in a managed shared house with other young internationals. The sweet spot for many Erasmus students: flexible, social, all-inclusive.
4.  **Classic flatshare (coloc)** — cheapest per room, but you handle bills, furniture, and finding trustworthy flatmates yourself.

## Why coliving suits Erasmus perfectly

An Erasmus semester is short (4–10 months), you arrive knowing no one, and you don't want to spend it doing admin. [Coliving](/actors) fits like a glove:

*   **Flexible leases** that match a semester — no 9-year Belgian contract.
*   **No guarantor drama** and simpler deposits (a common Erasmus headache — see [renting in Brussels](/blog/renting-in-brussels-guide)).
*   **All-inclusive** rent: bills, wifi, furniture, cleaning — one payment.
*   **Instant friends.** House dinners and apéros mean you're not eating alone in week one. For a short exchange, that community *is* the experience.
*   **Registration-friendly.** Staying over 3 months means you'll likely need to register at your commune; coliving contracts usually allow it.

## Best neighborhoods for Erasmus

*   **[Ixelles](/neighborhoods/ixelles)** — the student and expat heartland, near ULB/VUB, packed with bars (Place Flagey, Cimetière d'Ixelles). The classic Erasmus base.
*   **[Saint-Gilles](/neighborhoods/saint-gilles)** — artsy, central, a bit cheaper.

## How to book fast (and avoid scams)

1.  **Start early.** September and February intakes fill quickly — begin 6–8 weeks out.
2.  **Use managed operators** rather than random Facebook listings to avoid deposit scams. A professional coliving operator with real reviews is far safer.
3.  **Never pay a deposit before seeing the room** (photos, video tour, or ideally a viewing).
4.  **Confirm the semester end date** matches your lease with no penalty.
5.  **Ask about the vibe** — age, nationalities, how social the house is.

## The Erasmus survival tip

Say yes to everything in your first two weeks. The house dinner, the Plux Thursday, the day trip to [Ghent](/blog/brussels-day-trips-guide). Erasmus friendships form fast and burn bright — and living in a social [coliving house](/actors) puts you at the centre of it from the moment you drop your bags.
