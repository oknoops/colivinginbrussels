# Brussels Relocation Tips: 15 Things to Know Before You Move

Planning a move to the capital of Europe? These **Brussels relocation tips** cover the practical things newcomers wish they'd known earlier — the admin, the housing, the transport and the small cultural details — plus the common mistakes that cost people time and money. Read this before you book anything.

## Admin and paperwork

**1. You must register at your commune.** If you stay longer than three months, domiciliation (registration at your local commune) is required. Each commune has its own procedure and appointment system — check its official website early, as slots book up.

**2. Bring certified documents.** Belgian admin runs on paperwork. Bring copies of your birth certificate, diplomas and marriage certificate, ideally translated/legalised. Confirm requirements on official sources.

**3. Rules depend on your nationality.** EU citizens have an easier path than non-EU nationals. Don't rely on forums — verify visa and residence steps on the federal immigration office and your commune's official websites.

**4. Everything takes longer than expected.** Build in buffer time for appointments, ID cards and bank setup. Patience is a survival skill here.

## Housing

**5. Traditional flats often want a guarantor and long lease.** Classic Brussels rentals typically ask for a local guarantor, a multi-year lease and a big deposit — hard when you've just arrived.

**6. Coliving is the newcomer shortcut.** Furnished rooms, bills included, flexible 3–6 month leases, and **usually no Belgian guarantor needed**. All-in prices run roughly €500–€1,500/month. It's the easiest soft landing. Compare operators on the [rankings page](/actors).

**7. Pick your neighbourhood deliberately.** Areas vary hugely — social [Ixelles](/coliving/ixelles), creative [Saint-Gilles](/coliving/saint-gilles), EU-close [Etterbeek](/coliving/etterbeek), or leafy Uccle. Browse the [neighbourhoods overview](/neighborhoods) or take the [matchmaker quiz](/matchmaker).

**8. Know the real costs before you commit.** Check typical all-in rates on the [coliving prices guide](/coliving-brussels-prices) so you can budget accurately.

**9. Don't sign anything sight-unseen without verifying the operator.** Rental scams exist. Stick to verified operators and never wire deposits to unverified private landlords.

## Money and daily life

**10. You'll need a Belgian/EU bank account.** Most banks want proof of address and registration — another reason a real coliving address speeds things up. A digital bank can bridge the gap.

**11. Cards rule, cash is backup.** Bancontact and cards are used everywhere; keep a little cash for markets and small shops.

**12. Sort your health insurance.** Belgium's system runs on mandatory health funds (mutuelles/ziekenfonds). Enrol once registered and working, and keep private cover for the transition.

## Getting around and fitting in

**13. You may not need a car.** Brussels is compact with metro, tram, bus and a growing bike network. Many residents go car-free; test the commute from any home before committing.

**14. Learn a little French (and know it's bilingual).** Brussels is officially French/Dutch and very international. English works in most expat settings, but basic French smooths admin and daily errands, and always greet with "bonjour" before asking.

**15. Say yes early to build a social life.** Your first three months set your friend group. Coliving houses come with built-in community and events — a huge head start. See what's happening on the [What's On calendar](/whats-on).

## Common newcomer mistakes to avoid

- **Underestimating admin timelines** — start commune and bank steps immediately.
- **Signing a long, inflexible lease before you know the city** — stay flexible first.
- **Choosing a home purely on price** and ending up with a long, draining commute.
- **Wiring deposits to unverified landlords** — use trusted operators.

## FAQ

**Do I really need to register at the commune?**
Yes, if you stay longer than three months. Registration (domiciliation) is required, and the process differs by commune — check the official website of yours.

**Can I rent in Brussels without a Belgian guarantor?**
Yes — coliving generally doesn't require one, which is why it's so popular with newcomers. Confirm each operator's specific terms.

**How much should I budget for housing?**
All-in coliving typically runs €500–€1,500/month depending on area and room. See the [coliving prices guide](/coliving-brussels-prices) for details.
