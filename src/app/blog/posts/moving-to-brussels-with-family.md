# Moving to Brussels with Family: Areas, Schools & Housing

Moving to Brussels with family is a very different project from arriving solo. Instead of optimising for nightlife and a short commute, you're weighing green space, school runs, bedrooms, and a calm street to raise kids on. The good news: Brussels is genuinely family-friendly, compact, and packed with parks — and once you know which communes suit families, the search gets much simpler. Here's how to approach neighbourhoods, schools, and housing without the guesswork.

## The most family-friendly neighbourhoods

Brussels is a patchwork of 19 communes, each with its own character. For families, a few consistently rise to the top.

- **[Uccle](/neighborhoods/uccle)** — leafy, residential, and quiet, with big parks and a mix of houses and spacious flats. It's a long-standing favourite for families who want green streets and space to breathe.
- **[Woluwe-Saint-Lambert](/neighborhoods/woluwe-saint-lambert)** — greener, calmer, and well-served by parks and schools, popular with international families and EU staff.
- **[Etterbeek](/neighborhoods/etterbeek)** — more central and walkable while still family-workable, handy if one parent commutes to the EU quarter.

If you want to compare the full picture — vibe, transport, and who each area suits — browse all the [Brussels neighbourhoods](/neighborhoods) side by side before committing to a viewing.

## Schools: start early

Brussels offers three broad routes, and the right one depends on your timeline, budget, and how long you plan to stay.

- **International and private schools** follow English, French, or other national curricula (IB, British, American systems and more). They're the smoothest landing for families who move often, but places and fees vary widely.
- **European Schools** primarily serve EU-institution staff and follow the European Baccalaureate.
- **Local Belgian schools** (French-speaking or Dutch-speaking) are free or low-cost and are a wonderful path to integration and bilingualism — especially for younger children who pick up the language fast.

Two rules of thumb: apply as early as you possibly can, because popular schools fill up, and confirm current admission rules, catchment areas, and registration windows on the **official commune and school authority websites** rather than trusting hearsay. Requirements change year to year, so always check the primary source.

## Housing: why families usually want rentals, not coliving

Here's an honest note. Most coliving in Brussels is designed for individuals — young professionals, remote workers, and newcomers who want a private room plus shared common spaces and an instant community. It's a brilliant format for one person or a couple landing quickly, but it isn't built for families with children.

For a family, you'll almost always want a **standard unfurnished or furnished rental** — a house or an apartment with enough bedrooms, ideally near your chosen school and a park. A few things to know about the Belgian rental market:

- **Leases** are typically longer-term (the classic residential lease runs multiple years, though shorter terms exist). Read the term carefully before signing.
- **Domiciliation** — registering your address at the commune — is required when you stay longer than three months, and it's tied to school registration, healthcare, and residency admin. Handle it early.
- **Deposits and guarantees** for family rentals are usually more formal than for coliving. Budget for a rental guarantee and check what the landlord requires.

That said, coliving can still play a useful role: if you arrive before your family or need a soft landing while you house-hunt, a room in a coliving can buy you time in the city. Browse the [coliving operators](/actors) to see the range, or use the [pricing guide](/coliving-brussels-prices) to benchmark monthly costs before you compare against family rentals.

## A sensible first-month plan

1. Shortlist two or three neighbourhoods using the [neighbourhoods overview](/neighborhoods).
2. Contact schools and confirm admission windows on official sites.
3. Line up rental viewings near your top school choice.
4. Register your address (domiciliation) at the commune once you sign.
5. Explore weekend life and [what's on](/whats-on) so the kids feel at home fast.

## FAQ

**Is coliving suitable for families?**
Generally no — coliving is designed for individuals and couples, with private rooms and shared spaces. Families almost always want a dedicated house or apartment rental. Coliving can work as a short-term bridge while you house-hunt.

**Which Brussels neighbourhoods are best for families?**
Uccle and Woluwe-Saint-Lambert are top picks for green, quiet, residential living, while Etterbeek balances family life with a more central location.

**How do I register my children for school?**
Contact schools directly and check the official commune or school-authority website for current admission rules and registration deadlines. Apply as early as possible, as popular schools fill up quickly.
