# Monthly Budget in Brussels: What Newcomers Actually Spend

Before you move, the big question is simple: what are the real **monthly expenses in Brussels**? The honest answer is "it depends on your lifestyle," but that's not much help when you're trying to plan. So we've built three realistic monthly budgets — for a student, an intern and a young professional — using typical Brussels costs. Treat these as starting points, adjust for your own habits, and you'll arrive with a number you can actually trust.

## The building blocks of a Brussels budget

Almost everyone's monthly spend breaks down into the same categories:

- **Housing** — usually the biggest line by far.
- **Food** — groceries plus eating out.
- **Transport** — public transport pass, occasional taxis or bike.
- **Phone and internet** — a SIM plan (wifi is included in coliving).
- **Fun and extras** — going out, gym, subscriptions, the odd weekend trip.

Housing is where coliving quietly saves you money and stress: rooms run **€500–€1,500 per month all-in**, meaning utilities, wifi and communal cleaning are already baked into that single figure. That makes budgeting far more predictable than an empty flat with separate bills. See real ranges on our [coliving prices page](/coliving-brussels-prices).

## The student budget (lean)

A student watching every euro can keep things tight by choosing a budget coliving room or student housing and cooking at home.

- **Housing:** ~€500–€650 (a room with [Ikoab](/actors/ikoab) or [Colive](/actors/colive), or a student setup)
- **Food:** ~€200–€250 (mostly groceries, occasional cheap eats)
- **Transport:** low, thanks to heavily reduced youth public-transport passes
- **Phone:** ~€10–€15 on a budget SIM
- **Fun:** ~€80–€120

That lands most students in the rough range of **€850–€1,100 per month**. Our [student housing guide](/student-housing-brussels) digs into the cheapest ways to live well while studying.

## The intern budget (careful)

Interns and trainees — including the many EU-institution placements — often have a modest stipend and a fixed end date, so flexible housing and controlled spending matter.

- **Housing:** ~€600–€800 (a flexible coliving room near work, e.g. in [Etterbeek](/coliving/etterbeek) for the EU quarter)
- **Food:** ~€250–€300
- **Transport:** ~€35–€50 for a monthly pass (less with youth discounts)
- **Phone:** ~€15
- **Fun:** ~€120–€180

That's roughly **€1,050–€1,350 per month**. Coliving's no-guarantor, short-lease setup is a big plus when your placement only lasts a few months.

## The young professional budget (comfortable)

A working professional on a full salary can trade up: a bigger or better-located room, more meals out, and a bit more discretionary spending.

- **Housing:** ~€800–€1,200 (a premium room with an operator like [Cohabs](/actors/cohabs) or a private-leaning house)
- **Food:** ~€350–€450 (regular restaurants and cafés)
- **Transport:** ~€50 pass, plus the occasional taxi or shared bike
- **Phone:** ~€20–€30
- **Fun:** ~€250–€400 (nights out, gym, subscriptions, weekend trips)

That comes to around **€1,500–€2,100 per month**, with plenty of room to flex up or down.

## Easy ways to spend less

A few habits make a real difference:

1. **Pick all-in coliving** — one predictable bill beats juggling utility invoices, and it's often cheaper once you count furniture.
2. **Use youth transport discounts** if you qualify; they're substantial.
3. **Cook most nights** — groceries are far cheaper than eating out, and Brussels has great markets.
4. **Compare neighbourhoods** — living one district out can cut rent noticeably. See our [neighbourhoods guide](/neighborhoods).
5. **Chase free culture** — the city has plenty of no-cost events; check what's on via our [events page](/whats-on).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much money do I need per month to live in Brussels?**
As a rough guide: a lean student budget lands around €850–€1,100, an intern around €1,050–€1,350, and a comfortable young professional around €1,500–€2,100. Housing choice is the biggest swing factor.

**Is coliving cheaper than renting a studio?**
Often, yes — once you add furniture, utilities, wifi and setup costs to an empty studio, an all-in coliving room can work out cheaper, and it's far less hassle. Compare on our [prices page](/coliving-brussels-prices).

**What's the single biggest expense?**
Housing, almost always. It typically accounts for half or more of a newcomer's monthly budget, which is why getting it right matters most.
