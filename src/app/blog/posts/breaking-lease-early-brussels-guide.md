# Breaking a Lease or Leaving Your Coliving Early in Brussels: Notice Periods, Deposits, and Your Rights

Nobody signs a lease planning to break it. But plans in Brussels change for the most ordinary reasons: a job ends earlier than expected, a relationship doesn't survive the move, a new offer pulls you to another city, or a coliving house simply isn't the fit you hoped. Whatever the reason, the question is the same, and it's one worth understanding before it becomes urgent: what actually happens, legally and financially, when you need to leave before your contract says you can?

The short answer is that Brussels housing law gives tenants real, usable exit routes, and coliving contracts are often even more flexible than a traditional lease. The long answer depends on what kind of contract you signed, how far into it you are, and how much notice you're willing and able to give. Here's how it breaks down.

## First, know what kind of contract you actually have

Belgian residential leases in Brussels come in a few standard forms, and the rules for leaving early differ meaningfully between them.

- **The short-term lease (bail de courte durée / kortetermijnhuurovereenkomst)**, for a fixed period of three years or less. These are common for single tenants and smaller flatshares.
- **The standard nine-year lease (bail de neuf ans / negenjarige huurovereenkomst)**, the default for most unfurnished family and long-term rentals.
- **The coliving or furnished licence agreement**, which many operators use instead of a classic lease. These sit outside the strict residential lease law in some respects and are often genuinely easier to exit, see our guide on [what to ask before signing](/blog/coliving-brussels-what-to-ask-before-signing) if you haven't signed yet.

If you're not sure which you have, check the first page of the contract itself. It's usually stated explicitly, and it changes everything else in this guide.

## Leaving a standard lease early

For both the short-term and nine-year leases, Brussels tenancy law (part of Belgian federal and regional housing legislation) gives tenants a general right to end the lease early, with notice, even mid-term. The mechanics:

- **Notice period:** typically three months' written notice, regardless of how far into the lease you are.
- **Early-departure compensation:** if you leave during the first three years of a nine-year lease (or before the agreed term of a short-term lease), the landlord can usually charge a reducing penalty, often capped around one and a half, one, or half a month's rent depending on which year you're in. After the third year, this penalty typically disappears entirely, leaving just the notice period.
- **Form of notice:** always in writing, ideally by registered letter or at minimum a dated email with read confirmation, so there's no dispute later about when the clock started. This matters just as much for the [deposit you'll want back in full](/blog/brussels-rental-deposit-guide).

In practice, this means a nine-year lease is far less rigid than the name suggests. Three months' notice and, at worst, a modest penalty will get almost anyone out of almost any standard lease. What you can't generally do is simply stop paying and hand back the keys without notice, that exposes you to being billed for rent until a replacement tenant is found or the notice period would have ended, whichever comes first.

## Leaving a coliving contract early

Coliving operators set their own terms, which is both the appeal and the thing worth reading carefully before you sign. Most Brussels operators build in shorter, simpler notice periods than a classic lease, commonly:

- **One month's notice** for stays that have already run a few months, which is standard across much of the [coliving landscape](/actors).
- **A minimum stay commitment** (often one to three months) before you're free to give notice at all, common for operators targeting shorter stays or interns.
- **A smaller deposit** (frequently one to two months' rent) held directly by the operator rather than in a blocked bank account, which generally makes the financial wind-down faster too.

Because these are contractual terms rather than statutory ones, they vary company to company, and some operators are notably more flexible than others, particularly for documented reasons like a job relocation or a university placement ending. If your plans are even slightly uncertain, it's worth asking directly about early-exit terms during your search rather than after you've signed. If you're still comparing options, the [neighbourhood matchmaker](/matchmaker) and [coliving operator directory](/actors) are a good place to weigh flexibility alongside price and location.

## What giving notice looks like in practice

1. **Re-read your contract first.** Confirm the exact notice period, any minimum stay clause, and whether penalties scale down over time.
2. **Put it in writing immediately.** A registered letter is the gold standard for a classic lease; for coliving, a dated email to your house or community manager is usually sufficient, but check if your contract specifies a particular channel.
3. **Note the start date of your notice clearly.** Most disputes over early departures come down to disagreement about when notice was actually given, not whether it was valid.
4. **Ask about the financial breakdown in writing**, specifically, any penalty owed, the final rent due date, and how the deposit return process will work once you're out.
5. **Plan your état des lieux de sortie (move-out inventory)** for your last few days, the same document that governs [getting your deposit back](/blog/brussels-rental-deposit-guide).
6. **Keep every email.** If anything is disputed later, a clear paper trail showing when you gave notice and what was agreed is almost always what resolves it.

## Subletting as a middle path

If leaving entirely isn't necessary, just a temporary absence, subletting (sous-location / onderverhuring) is sometimes an option, but only with the landlord's or operator's written consent. Subletting without permission is a serious breach that can void your protections entirely, so never treat it as a workaround to avoid giving proper notice. Ask explicitly rather than assuming it's allowed, especially for a short absence like an internship abroad or a few months back home.

## When the house, not the contract, is the problem

Sometimes the issue isn't your plans but the living situation itself, a flatmate conflict, a house that isn't being maintained, or simply a mismatch you didn't anticipate before moving in. That's a different problem from the logistics above, but the same principle applies: document issues in writing as they happen, raise them with the landlord or operator directly, and don't wait for things to become unbearable before acting. Choosing well the first time matters more than any exit clause, which is exactly what a longer look at [Brussels neighbourhoods](/neighborhoods) and the available operators is meant to help with before you sign anything.

## If a dispute arises

Most early-departure situations resolve through a straightforward written exchange: you give notice, the other side confirms the penalty (if any) and the final dates, and the deposit follows the usual process. When it doesn't resolve that easily, Brussels tenants can bring a dispute to the **justice of the peace (juge de paix / vredegerechter)** for their commune at no great cost and without needing a lawyer, or seek free initial guidance from a tenants' rights organisation. Keep your written notice and every follow-up email ready; in practice, that paper trail decides almost every case.

## The bottom line

Leaving a Brussels lease or coliving contract early is rarely as locked-in as the paperwork makes it sound. The nine-year lease is an exit with three months' notice and a shrinking penalty, not a nine-year sentence, and most coliving agreements are built for exactly the kind of life changes that make people need to leave. The part that actually matters is doing it properly: read the clause, give written notice with a clear date, and keep the paper trail. Do that, and leaving early is paperwork, not a crisis.
